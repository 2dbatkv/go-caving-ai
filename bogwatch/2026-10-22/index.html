What this call was

A different kind of call

The first three BOG Watch calls were close readings of the record: the minutes, the rulebook, the Constitution and the financials. This one was different. It put the Board's 2026 agendas on screen and asked the people on the call what they think the Board should be working on, and what it has done well. The answers become the starting point for a questionnaire to cavers across North America.

Five people took part. The call ran about ninety minutes.

Part 1

The record

  • What the Board put on its agendas in 2026, January to the 17 October meeting: one table, nine meetings, open-session motions beside what was reported from closed session.
  • By theme: about $535,000 committed by motion (Mill Creek legal costs $30k, Cave Avenue demolition up to $60k, a museum note of $190k later rescinded, $100k each to NCRC and VTC, $55k for the Executive Director search); roughly 48 motions in July rewriting the fiscal Acts; four land and partner agreements; seven people items.
  • Not on any 2026 open-session agenda: Article III of the Constitution, the missing FY2022 Form 990, publication of an audit, an evaluation framework for the Executive Director, membership numbers, communication with members.
  • Since the last call: the Conflict of Interest Policy, posted the day before the 17 October vote, and the agenda for that meeting, re-posted the night before with one new closed-session item.

The one limit, stated at the start: the record shows what reached an agenda or a vote. Committee, staff and closed-session work does not appear. Sources are on the slides and on the home page under “What they're up to now”.

Part 2

What attendees said should be on the list

In the order raised. Each is an attendee's view.

Caves and land

More NSS preserves and acquisitions as the Society's resources grow; more MOUs and cooperative agreements of the kind approved in April (US Forest Service, Appalachian Cave Conservancy, Thunderhole). One suggestion: let members vote on which of three candidate caves becomes the next preserve. Cooperation with the Southeastern Cave Conservancy was singled out as something members would welcome more of.

A welcoming community for new cavers

The structure the NSS and its grottos give a nineteen- or twenty-year-old trying to get involved: connections, landowner relations, something safe and dependable. This was the one priority named on the call that appears nowhere on the 2026 agendas, except perhaps under the Code of Conduct report.

Transparency

  • Is it important to members to know what the Board is doing, and how much detail do they want? The $190,000 museum note, approved in March and rescinded in June with no public explanation of either decision, was the example.
  • Member comments at Board meetings could be entered in the minutes with the speaker's consent, so other members can see what was asked. That question is not currently asked.
  • Where a matter must stay confidential (litigation, personnel), the cost could still be reported. One attendee, from experience as a grotto officer, explained why some legal matters cannot be discussed in open session; the question that followed was whether the amounts, at least, could be.
  • Cost breakdowns for large items, such as the $30,000 approved in January for legal costs.

Who serves on the Board, and how they are chosen

  • What qualifications does the Nominating Committee look for when it asks someone to run? One attendee suggested simply asking the committee.
  • Whether there should be minimum requirements for directors, given the size of the Society's assets and budget. The counter-argument was also made: the Presidency of the United States has two qualifications, and the Society's rules deliberately let any adult member in good standing run.
  • One attendee's summary, which the room agreed with: the requirements for serving on the Board have not kept pace with the sophistication of the business the Board now conducts.
  • Whether the membership should have a larger part in choosing the Executive Director.

Records and rules

Complete the Board Manual so that the job descriptions for the Secretary-Treasurer, the officers and the directors are in it; today those appendices are blank. Correct errors in agendas and minutes on a timely basis (the 17 October agenda calls Act 03-445 a Bylaw). Version a document when it changes: both copies of the 17 October agenda are labelled V 1.0.

Volunteers

Volunteer service was recognised throughout. The point made was that willingness is necessary but not sufficient for work of this scale, and that volunteers should be enabled to contribute expertise, including through reimbursement.

The Conflict of Interest Policy

One attendee read the clause “a conflict exists only if the Chair of the Board and/or the Executive Director decides” alongside the fact that the Interim Executive Director is also the Secretary-Treasurer, and asked how a conflict concerning either of those offices would be decided.

Part 2, continued

What attendees said the Board has done well

The land and partner agreements: the two MOUs and the joint management agreement approved in April, and the Mill Creek Sink work. Several people said protecting caves, including the legal costs that come with it, is what they most want the Society's money spent on.

Next

Where the questionnaire goes from here

There was agreement that a questionnaire is worth doing, for three reasons given on the call:

  1. A call of five people is not representative of cavers at large.
  2. Most members, by their own account, do not follow what the Board does closely enough to grade it. So the questionnaire should ask what people value and what they would want to know, not ask them to judge a record they have not read.
  3. Five or ten voices are easy to set aside; a hundred or a thousand are not.

Design points raised: keep it short and pointed; make it open to cavers whether or not they are NSS members, but record membership so results can be read either way; publish the method and the number of responses with the results.

A draft will go to the attendees for comment first. Results will be presented on a later call and published here. To hear when it goes out, join the notification list.

Looked up during the call

Facts read from the record

These were looked up live. Sources: the Board of Governors Manual as of 6 July 2026, and the minutes named.

  • Agendas

    A preliminary agenda is placed on a public area of the NSS website at least one month before a meeting and replaced by the final agenda at least two weeks before, “with every effort to have all motions presented” (Appendix, Section II). Every agenda must include an open discussion section (Acts 04-093, 04-472).

  • Nominating Committee

    At least three members, appointed annually by the Chair of the Board and approved by the Board (Bylaws V.B.4). It runs the director election and, since the 2024 Bylaws, identifies Executive Director candidates and oversees the search firm (VI.A.3, VI.E.3). The search must use a third-party firm with a candidate guarantee of six months to a year (Appendix X, Part II). The last public roster is from 2024: Anna Pearce, chair, with three members. No Board approval of the committee appears in the 2025 or 2026 minutes.

  • Elections

    Three directors are elected by members each year for three-year terms and seated at the Convention Board meeting; a fourth is appointed by the Board at its spring meeting. Working back from a June 2027 Convention in Texas: ballots close about mid-May, go out by mid-April, nominations close about 1 March, and the call for nominations goes out by mid-January 2027. Any eligible member can be placed on the ballot by petition of 25 voting members; write-ins are allowed.

  • 2020 committee

    The Executive Director Exploratory Committee concluded that governance issues had to be resolved before an ED was hired, that an ED “would have no control over the governance components of the society by design and therefore cannot fix them,” and that any candidate “should start the job knowing how they will be evaluated.” Its report was delivered in July 2020 and the committee dissolved in November 2020.
