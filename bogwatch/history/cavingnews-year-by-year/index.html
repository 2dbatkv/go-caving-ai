Caving News, year by year

Eleven articles, two for each year the site published and the one article of 2016, as the site published them.

Not affiliated with the NSS. A historical account built only from public records: NSS Board documents, the Internet Archive's capture index, archived front pages and domain registries. No forum post, member profile or reader comment was read, and none is reproduced.

Compiled: 2026-10-09. Companion to 2026-10-09_cavingnews-history_findings.md. Ground rules: Public only · Descriptive · Traceable. The articles are published journalism on an open website. Reader comments were not read. The only person named is the site's owner (publisher); guest authors and commenters are referred to by role.

How the pages were obtained. The live site (cavingnews.com) was unreachable on the morning of 2026-10-09 (Cloudflare 521, origin not responding), so each article was viewed in the Wayback Machine in a browser on Aaron's computer, at the earliest successful capture listed in the index (data/cavingnews_cdx.jsonl), and screenshotted by hand. Files: raw/cavingnews/screenshots/<article date>_<slug>_wb<capture date>[_n].png. Three captures (Jan 2012, Mar 2012, Mar 2014) were archived without their stylesheet and render as bare HTML; the text is complete. Alternative styled captures exist and are listed in the notes.

How the eleven were chosen. From the 1,831 article addresses in the index, two per year 2011–2015 and the one article of 2016, favouring stories that (a) mark a milestone in the site's own life, (b) show the NSS relationship, or (c) report a landmark in caving that the site covered as news. Headlines are the site's own.

2011

1. Welcome to Caving News (12 March 2011). Capture 2012-01-07. Three sentences: "Hi everyone! Welcome to Caving News! A new site dedicated to cavers, spelunkers and potholers around the world. Caving news aims to be the best source for quality cave news on the internet," and an invitation to send in news. Tagline on the masthead: "Daily Cave News for Cavers, by Cavers!" The studio mark "Curious Concept" in the corner identifies the developer's own brand. By the January 2012 capture the sidebar already offered "Year in Review: The Top Caving News of 2011" and the site had posted about its own milestones ("Caving News Gets New Look", "Caving News is Six Months Old Today"). Categories: Discoveries, Events, Expeditions, Excavations, Conservation, Biospeleology, Search & Rescue, Incidents. Files: 2011-03-12_welcome-to-caving-news_wb20120107{,_top,_bottom}.png.

2. Cavers Discuss White-Nose Syndrome at NSS Convention (25 July 2011). Capture 2012-01-06. Report of the Wednesday session "White Nose Syndrome, A Western Perspective" at the Glenwood Springs convention. The NSS WNS liaison is quoted calling evidence of human spread "speculation and hypotheses"; a Fish and Wildlife Service representative is quoted that "there is strong evidence that the fungus that causes the disease can be transported by humans." The site's own voice follows: cavers "question the policy of closing caves as a preventative measure"; closures "are only effective at keeping the responsible cavers out." Source credited: Post Independent. Related strip: Congress approves $4 million for WNS; USFWS national plan; Nova Scotia and Maine confirmations. Files: 2011-07-25_cavers-discuss-wns-at-nss-convention_wb20120106_{1,2,3}.png.

2012

3. New Estimate: Up to 6.7 Million Bats Killed by WNS (20 January 2012). Capture 2012-01-24 (unstyled; styled alternative /web/20120127044802/). USFWS biologists now estimate "at least 5.7 million to 6.7 million bats" dead since 2006, replacing the "more than a million" figure "restated thousands of times since 2009"; the estimate came out of the Northeast Bat Working Group meeting in Carlisle, Pennsylvania (140 biologists). Source line: "U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service via NSS White Nose Syndrome Page", two months before the MOU. Related: "Poll: Were you Alarmed by the New WNS Death Toll Estimate?", one of the site's reader polls. Files: 2012-01-20_6-7-million-bats-killed-by-wns_wb20120124_unstyled_{1,2,3}.png.

4. Caving News Joins Forces with National Speleological Society (12 March 2012). Capture 2012-03-14 (unstyled; styled alternative /web/20121114085251/). "On the first birthday of Caving News, we are happy to announce that we have joined forces with the National Speleological Society." Goals: "to introduce more people around the world to interesting news about caves and caving, as well as to increase the visibility of each organization," and "bringing on new content writers." Announces the Caving News Widget "to be featured on the NSS website", and "not restricted to the NSS website, it is available for anyone to put on their very own site." Board side: MOU approved 31 March 2012 (1203BOG.pdf item 19), signed by the owner 10 June 2012. File: 2012-03-12_caving-news-joins-forces-with-nss_wb20120314_unstyled.png.

2013

5. This Week on CaveChat: January 5th–11th, 2013 Edition (11 January 2013). Capture 2013-01-15. Masthead now reads "IN ASSOCIATION WITH THE" beside the NSS seal; the column is tagged "NSS". Standing intro: "CaveChat.org is the official forum of the National Speleological Society. Since its creation in 2005 CaveChat has quickly become the go to place for everything related to caving. With more than 5,800 members and over 112,000 posts…" Five thread titles follow (gloves for wet vertical caving, flowstone questions, a cave/spring find, Tyvek water protection, 3D walk-through cave map technology). The author box is the CaveChat administrator's own description of his role and of Onlinecavers.com. The CaveChat yellow-bat logo appears on the related-news tiles. Sidebar: 2,500 followers; "Ask Cavers" series. Files: 2013-01-11_this-week-on-cavechat_wb20130115_{1,2}.png.

6. Kentucky's Mammoth Cave Hits 400 Miles (16 February 2013). Capture 2013-02-22. Announced at the Mammoth Cave Research Symposium by the park superintendent and the Cave Research Foundation president; 400 miles (643 km); the growth "a matter of incremental additions … rather than a single major discovery"; CRF volunteers 10,669 hours in 2012. Photo: NPS. Sidebar: 2,700 followers. Files: 2013-02-16_mammoth-cave-hits-400-miles_wb20130222_{1,2}.png.

2014

7. Virginia's New River Cave Becomes 14th NSS Cave Preserve (17 March 2014). Capture 2014-03-17 (unstyled; styled alternative /web/20140326012748/). "The NSS Board of Governors, meeting this weekend in Richmond, Virginia, voted to add a 14th preserve to the NSS Cave Preserve system—the five-mile-long New River Cave, longest cave in Giles County, Virginia. It is the first cave preserve for the society in the commonwealth." Photo "via NSS". Published the same weekend as the vote, in a month with five articles in total: the MOU's liaison clause in practice. Files: 2014-03-17_new-river-cave-14th-nss-preserve_wb20140317_unstyled_{1,2}.png.

8. Real Explorers Don't Use Cable Cars: Save Son Doong (1 December 2014). Capture 2014-12-08. A long first-person guest essay by a Vietnam Caves Project expedition caver: why he caves, the 2007 descent of Vuc Tang ("255 meter … deep vertical entrance pitch"), Hang En and Son Doong, and the case against the proposed cable car, ending with the

SaveSonDoong petition. The new section navigation (Science, Conservation, Exploration, Community, Tourism, Rescue) is

in place; 4,300 followers. Follow-up in the index: "Son Doong saved: cable car, no development until 2030" (2015-02-13). Files: 2014-12-01_save-son-doong_wb20141208_{1,2,3}.png.

2015

9. Missouri Mayor Welcomes Cavers to the 2015 NSS Convention (23 January 2015). Capture 2015-02-02 (the only capture). The Waynesville mayor's first official release for the convention: 2,000 cavers expected, "over 6,300 caves" in Missouri, Pulaski County "over 300", Roubidoux Spring; convention 13–17 July. Ticker: "Get the Caving News Widget!"; 4,500 followers. The related strip is a short history of NSS conventions as the site covered them: 2011 Glenwood Springs, 2013 Shippensburg, 2016 Ely announced, the Center for Biological Diversity dispute of 2011. Files: 2015-01-23_missouri-mayor-welcomes-2015-nss-convention_wb20150202_{1,2}.png.

10. Jewel Cave Surpasses 175 Miles in Length (21 February 2015). Capture 2015-02-23. Two teams at West Camp, 13–16 February, surveyed 6,557.65 ft (1,998.7 m); total 175.13 miles (281.8 km); depth to 677 ft; "over 100 unexplored leads"; third longest behind Mammoth (400 mi) and Sac Actun (206.9 mi). Includes the NPS line plot of the new survey (new passage in red). 4,600 followers. Files: 2015-02-21_jewel-cave-surpasses-175-miles_wb20150223_{1,2}.png.

2016

11. Video: Cavers Continue to Push Sweden's Longest Underwater Cave (21 April 2016). Capture 2016-04-21 (same day). Four lines around an embedded video from the 2016 Bjurälven expedition: a folding aluminium ladder "to explore the roof inside 'the rain hall'"; Dolinsjö Cave extended 353 m to 2,135 m (1.3 miles). "[via Micke Tilja]" credits the source. 5,000 followers; the widget banner still runs. Nothing on the page suggests it is the last; the newest story on every later front page, through October 2026, is this one. The embedded video no longer plays from the archived page; a frame from the expedition's video on YouTube is saved alongside. Files: 2016-04-21_swedens-longest-underwater-cave_wb20160421_{1,2}.png, ..._youtube-still.png.

What the eleven show together

  • The site's own arc in its masthead: "Daily Cave News for Cavers, by Cavers!" with the Curious Concept mark (2011); "IN ASSOCIATION WITH THE" NSS seal from 2012 to the end; followers 2,500 (Jan 2013) → 2,700 → 4,300 → 4,500 → 4,600 → 5,000 (Apr 2016), still rising after publication stopped.
  • The NSS relationship was content, not just a logo: WNS material "via NSS" before the MOU; the CaveChat column and NSS News previews during the thin months; Board decisions and convention releases reported within days.
  • White-nose syndrome is the thread from the first year to the last front page (Idaho Fish and Game "enlists cavers in WNS battle", April 2016).
  • Three kinds of piece: aggregated news with a source line, the site's own editorial voice (the 2011 closures passage), and guest essays (2014).
