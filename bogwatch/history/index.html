Not affiliated with the NSS. A historical account built only from public records: NSS Board documents, the Internet Archive's capture index, archived front pages and domain registries. No forum post, member profile or reader comment was read, and none is reproduced.

How these were made

Public record, and only that

Four kinds of source: NSS Board minutes, agendas and officer reports posted on caves.org; the Internet Archive's public index of captures (dates, addresses and status codes); archived copies of each site's own front page, for section names and the running totals shown to every visitor; and the public domain registries. Every request was read-only, identified by name and contact, rate-limited and logged.

Deliberately not read: any forum topic, member profile, member list, search page or reader comment. Those carry members' names, comments and opinions. Usernames printed on a front page were blanked before anything was written. Volunteers are referred to by role; Board members and officers appear as the Society's own minutes name them.

CaveChat, 2005–2026

The NSS discussion forum

Founded 1 September 2005 and formalised by the Board that November, CaveChat grew to 6,000 members and 116,000 posts by 2013, faded to a few dozen posts a year, was retired on paper in March 2021 with a promise to archive it for members, and ran for four more years until it went dark on 28 March 2025. Its domain changed registrar on 28 September 2026. The record does not say why it went dark, where the promised archive is, or who holds the name now.

Caving News, 2011–2026

The NSS's media partner

A one-person daily cave-news site that published 1,831 articles between March 2011 and April 2016. The Board made it an official partner by Memorandum of Understanding in 2012 and put its feed on the caves.org home page; the MOU was rescinded in 2023, seven years after the last article. The site returned server errors from November 2021 to April 2025 and was quietly restored in August 2025, where it remains, with its 2016 content and the NSS logo still on its masthead.

Why this is here

Context for the record

Both channels were the Society's own: one it owned and retired, one it partnered with and let lapse. Each account ends with questions the public record cannot answer and the Society's records could. They are offered as history, not as a charge: what was built, what it did, and what became of it.
